Research structure

Work packages

BATT-AI is organised into four connected work packages that progress from grid-capacity modelling and market-aware battery participation to inertia provision and power hardware-in-the-loop validation.

WP1Grid capacity

Grid connection capacity optimisation

This work package develops a dynamic grid-connection capacity model and a market mechanism for sharing available capacity. It investigates how artificial intelligence can quantify and optimise connection limits as local grid conditions change, helping battery operators use available capacity while relieving distribution-grid congestion.

The research combines an AI-empowered capacity optimisation model with a connection-capacity trading mechanism. Together, these methods aim to coordinate access to constrained grid connections and make flexible capacity available to local users.

Researchers: Nataly Bañol Arias and Riccardo Toffanin (University of Twente).

Key partners:Delft University of Technology – Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Mathematics and Computer ScienceUniversity of TwenteStedinENTRNCE
WP2Markets

Optimal multi-market participation

This work package develops a decision-support tool for battery participation across multiple electricity markets. It studies how operators can coordinate market decisions while accounting for uncertainty, operating constraints and the effects of battery use on long-term performance.

The tool uses model predictive control, which updates operating decisions as new information becomes available. A data-driven battery degradation model captures ageing and state-of-health impacts, helping assess the trade-off between immediate market revenue and the cost of battery wear.

Researchers: Kenneth Bruninx and Peter Braun (Delft University of Technology).

Key partners:Delft University of Technology – Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Mathematics and Computer ScienceDelft University of Technology – Faculty of Technology, Policy and ManagementEnecoKYOS
WP3Inertia

Inertia market participation

This work package assesses the operational value and contracting strategies for battery energy storage systems providing inertia services to support grid stability. It examines the operational and economic effects of inertia provision, including battery response power and degradation costs.

The research develops market participation strategies, operational guidelines and long-term contracting approaches. It also studies how inertia provision can be combined with other battery services, supporting decisions about long-term capacity allocation and service-stacking optimisation.

Researcher: Yichao Zhang (Delft University of Technology).

Key partners:Delft University of Technology – Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Mathematics and Computer ScienceAlfen
WP4Validation

Demonstration and validation of battery models and market mechanisms

This work package demonstrates and validates battery degradation models, market mechanisms and economic models for battery energy storage systems. It connects the methods developed in WP1, WP2 and WP3 with practical case studies and testing to assess their operational feasibility and economic value.

It validates capacity optimisation models using a real case study, considering practical grid-connection limits and battery operating constraints. It tests parallel participation in multiple markets under field conditions to assess the feasibility and economic performance of the proposed strategies. It also validates economic models for assessing the value of battery-provided inertia services.
