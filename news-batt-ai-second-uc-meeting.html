BATT-AI Second User Committee Meeting: Research Progress and Industry Collaboration

Researchers and industrial partners reviewed progress across the project's three research work packages and identified priorities for the next phase.

On 4 September 2026, the BATT-AI consortium gathered for its second User Committee (UC) meeting to present the progress made across its research work packages and exchange perspectives with industrial partners. The meeting provided an opportunity to review ongoing activities, discuss research challenges, and identify priorities for the next phase of the project.

The programme included an update from the Dutch Research Council (NWO), an overview of project management activities, and presentations from the three research work packages. Discussions focused on the technical and economic challenges of large-scale battery energy storage systems (BESS), particularly their role in congestion management, electricity markets, and inertia provision.

WP1 – Network Congestion Management and Capacity Trading

The University of Twente team presented its ongoing research on grid connection capacity optimisation and local capacity trading mechanisms.

The work focuses on improving the utilisation of distribution network capacity through risk- and uncertainty-aware optimisation, while considering operational constraints and fairness among network users. Another research direction involves the development of local capacity markets to facilitate more efficient allocation of available connection capacity.

The team also outlined its plans to further develop the proposed models and assess their applicability to realistic Dutch distribution networks in collaboration with industrial partners.

WP2 – Optimal Battery Participation in Electricity Markets

The TU Delft team presented its research on model predictive control (MPC)-based decision support for battery participation in electricity markets.

Current activities focus on developing optimisation strategies for continuous intraday trading, accounting for market dynamics, battery operating constraints, and the interaction between financial trading decisions and physical battery operation.

The presentation also addressed the next steps towards more realistic market modelling and the integration of additional electricity market services into the decision-making framework.

WP3 – Inertia Market Participation of Battery Energy Storage Systems

The TU Delft team introduced its ongoing research on the participation of battery energy storage systems in inertia markets.

The research examines the operational requirements and economic implications of inertia provision, including battery operating constraints, market availability requirements, and contracting arrangements.

Another focus is the integration of inertia provision with other battery applications, particularly energy arbitrage. The team outlined further research directions related to optimal contracting strategies and the economic assessment of multi-service battery operation.

Research Collaboration and Next Steps

Beyond the work package presentations, academic researchers and industrial partners discussed the practical relevance of the ongoing research, opportunities for knowledge transfer and collaboration, and priorities for the next project phase. The meeting also addressed plans for dissemination, publications, and the potential application of research outcomes in industrial settings.

The second User Committee meeting marked an important opportunity to align ongoing research activities with the needs of industry partners and strengthen collaboration across the BATT-AI consortium. The project will continue advancing its research on congestion management, optimal battery market participation, and inertia services, with an emphasis on the practical application of the developed methods.
