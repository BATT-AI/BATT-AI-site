Project overview

The project

01

Why BATT-AI

Power grids are increasingly constrained by electrification, renewable integration and the rapid growth of distributed energy resources. Grid congestion is becoming a structural challenge: demand for connection capacity is rising faster than conventional grid reinforcement can be delivered, while expanding network infrastructure is costly, time-consuming and often limited by permitting, planning and spatial constraints.

At the same time, the growing share of converter-interfaced renewable generation reduces the natural inertia traditionally provided by synchronous generators. This makes power systems more sensitive to disturbances and increases the need for fast, flexible resources that can support stable operation, especially in weak or heavily loaded grids.

Large-scale battery energy storage systems can help address both challenges. When operated intelligently, batteries can relieve local congestion, provide fast active-power support and contribute synthetic inertia. However, these capabilities only become scalable if battery operation also makes economic sense. BATT-AI therefore focuses on AI-enabled control and decision support that allow batteries to participate effectively and profitably in electricity markets while supporting grid congestion management and system stability. By aligning technical grid needs with market-based incentives, the project helps accelerate the deployment of storage as a practical tool for a more reliable and flexible power system.

02

What we build

  • Congestion-aware operation: control strategies that respect dynamic connection limits and make flexibility available where it matters most.
  • AI-enabled decision support: optimisation tools for multi-market participation that explicitly consider uncertainty and degradation-driven cost trade-offs.
  • Inertia market participation: strategies for evaluating inertia value, service-stacking opportunities, and optimal participation in inertia market.
  • Validation and demonstration: integrated testing with realistic data and power hardware-in-the-loop to bridge research and practice.
03

How we work

The project combines modelling, optimisation and experimental validation. We connect grid constraints, market mechanisms and battery physics in a unified workflow so that operational strategies remain feasible, interpretable and deployable.

04

Expected outcomes

  • Analytical methods and software prototypes to support congestion relief by exploiting battery flexibility in distribution networks.
  • Market participation strategies, operational guidelines and contracting frameworks for batteries providing inertia services.
  • A multi-market MPC-based coordination framework enabling batteries to participate in congestion management, inertia markets and energy arbitrage simultaneously.
  • Stakeholder-oriented insights on technical feasibility, economic value and operational requirements for TSOs, DSOs, aggregators, battery owners and technology providers.
