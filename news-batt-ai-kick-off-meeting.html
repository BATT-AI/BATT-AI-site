BATT-AI Project Kick-off Meeting at TU Delft

Researchers and representatives from the project's academic and industrial partners met to establish a shared direction for BATT-AI.

The BATT-AI project held its official kick-off meeting on 3 February 2026 at Delft University of Technology (TU Delft), bringing together researchers and representatives from the project's academic and industrial partners.

Funded by the Netherlands Organisation for Scientific Research (NWO) under the Open Technology Programme, BATT-AI focuses on improving the utilisation and economic viability of large-scale battery energy storage systems (BESS) through artificial intelligence, optimisation, and advanced control.

The project addresses three interconnected research challenges:

  • Grid congestion management: Developing dynamic grid connection capacity models and market mechanisms to improve the utilisation of existing distribution networks.
  • Optimal multi-market participation: Developing AI-enabled decision support tools that account for electricity market uncertainty, battery degradation, and operational constraints.
  • Inertia market participation: Assessing the economic viability of battery inertia provision and developing optimal contracting strategies to maximise revenue through multi-service participation.

During the meeting, the consortium discussed the overall project objectives, research activities across the work packages, and opportunities for collaboration between academic researchers and industrial partners.

The discussions also addressed the importance of connecting theoretical developments with practical applications, including realistic grid conditions, electricity market requirements, and battery operating limitations.

The kick-off established a common direction for the project and initiated collaboration among TU Delft, the University of Twente, Stedin, Alfen, Eneco, ENTRNCE, and KYOS.

Through these activities, BATT-AI aims to support the deployment of large-scale batteries as flexible and economically viable assets in future electricity networks.
